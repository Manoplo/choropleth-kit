choropleth-kit has **three API levels over one implementation**. They are not three libraries: each level is built from the one below, so moving from one to the next never means starting over.

| Level        | You write                          | You get                                                  | You take over                              |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Preset**   | `<ChoroplethMap>`                  | Everything wired: map, legend, tooltip, zoom, drill-down | Nothing; you configure it with props       |
| **Compound** | `<Choropleth.Root>` and its pieces | The same pieces, in the order and place you choose       | Layout: what is rendered, where and how    |
| **Headless** | `useChoropleth()`                  | Geometry, colors, legend data and event props            | Every element: you paint each path yourself |

Start with the preset. Drop down only when you hit something it cannot express.

## What the preset actually is

The preset has no logic of its own. It is exactly `Root` plus `Svg`, `Legend` and `Tooltip`, and it adds `Markers`, `Breadcrumb`, `ZoomControls` and `GestureHint` when your props ask for them. If you need another layout, you can copy it and change the pieces. That is the whole idea of the compound level.

## The compound pieces

All of them live under `Choropleth`, and they read from the `Root` around them.

| Piece                        | What it is                                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Choropleth.Root`            | The container. It takes **all the options** (data, variant, theme, zoom…). It is a positioned box and exposes the `ref` handle. |
| `Choropleth.Svg`             | The `<svg>`. With no children it renders the default layers (below).            |
| `Choropleth.Legend`          | The legend. It is HTML, so it goes next to the `<svg>`, not inside it.          |
| `Choropleth.Tooltip`         | The tooltip. Pass a render function as its child to replace the content.        |
| `Choropleth.Breadcrumb`      | Drill-down navigation.                                                          |
| `Choropleth.ZoomControls`    | The `+`, `−` and reset buttons.                                                 |
| `Choropleth.GestureHint`     | The "Ctrl + scroll to zoom" hint for `wheel: "cooperative"`.                    |
| `Choropleth.Marker`, `Markers` | HTML pins and cards anchored to `[lon, lat]`. HTML, so they sit above the SVG. |

**Inside `Choropleth.Svg`**, you can pass your own children to choose the SVG layers. The defaults, in order, are: `Defs`, then a `Viewport` containing `DrillLayers` (context), `Regions`, `Patterns`, `Borders`, `Bubbles`, `FallbackPoints`, `Selection`, `Labels` and `DrillLayers` (exiting).

:::caution
If you pass children to `Choropleth.Svg`, they **replace** the defaults. Include the layers you still want, and keep them inside `Choropleth.Viewport`, the group that receives the camera transform.
:::

## Headless: what the hook returns

`useChoropleth` takes the same options as the other levels and returns what you need to render:

- `containerRef`, `width`, `height`: put the ref on a container so the map adapts to its width, and use the size for the `viewBox`.
- `regions`: each with its `id`, `path` (the `d` attribute), `fill`, `datum`, `value` and `formattedValue`.
- `borders`: the interior borders as one path, or `null` with GeoJSON.
- `legend` (and `sizeLegend`): the legend items, as data.
- `bubbles`, `fallbackPoints`, `regionLabels`: the other layers, as data.
- `getRegionProps(region)`: the event props (`onClick`, `onPointerEnter`, `onPointerLeave`) to spread on each `<path>`. Without it, hover, click and tooltips will not work.
- `store`: the interaction store, to read hover or the camera with `useMapStore`.
- `drillNav`, `status`, `scale`, `joinReport` and more.

Keep `geography` at a **stable reference** (a module constant, or `useMemo`): the hook memoizes the geometry by its identity.

## Which one should I use?

- **Preset:** you want a good map quickly, and props are enough. Most cases.
- **Compound:** you need a different layout (legend somewhere else, no tooltip, an extra layer) or your own tooltip, legend or controls, but you still want the camera, drill-down and theming.
- **Headless:** you need to control every element: a custom renderer, an unusual design system, or output that is not the stock SVG. You give up the built-in camera and components, and add what you need.

## Moving between levels

- **Options stay the same.** `data`, `getId`, `getValue`, `variant`, `palette`, `drill`, `zoom` and the callbacks are the same props on `ChoroplethMap`, `Choropleth.Root` and `useChoropleth`.
- **The handle stays the same.** `ref` on `ChoroplethMap` or `Choropleth.Root`, and `useChoroplethApi()` inside the compound, give you the same `zoomTo`, `drillDown` and friends.
- **Small defaults travel with the preset.** For example, the preset passes `emptyLabel` so "No data" appears in the legend; with `Choropleth.Root` you set `emptyLabel` yourself, as the compound example above does.
- **What changes** is who renders. Going from preset to compound, move the props that configure the legend, tooltip, breadcrumb, zoom controls and markers onto the matching piece.
- Presentation props such as `theme`, `classNames` and `unstyled` belong to the preset and compound levels. In headless you style your own elements.

See [Theming contract](/concepts/theming/) for styling, and [How a map is built](/concepts/how-a-map-is-built/) for what every level shares.
